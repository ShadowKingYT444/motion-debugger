The science

Sleep is not one state. It is a loop of four, repeated about every ninety minutes, and each part needs a different sound.

StageShare of the nightWhat the brain is doingWhat Drift plays
N1Drowsy
~5%

Alpha waves fade and the edges of thought loosen. A noise can wake you without you ever noticing.

Rain, close and warm. The breath tone fades out.

N2Light
45–50%

Sleep spindles appear and heart rate slows. It is the longest stage of the night.

Low pad and brown noise. The bright layers thin out.

N3Deep
15–20%

Slow waves dominate and the body repairs. You are hardest to wake and easiest to disturb without knowing.

Almost nothing. Steady masking that swells only to cover a spike.

REMDreaming
20–25%

Vivid dreams and fast eye movement. A light sleep that is easy to leave.

Open, airy textures. No pulses and no pacing.

  • 90min

    Typical length of one sleep cycle. It runs from 70 to 120 across people, which is why Drift learns yours.

  • 4–6

    Cycles in a night. The early ones are deep-heavy, the last ones mostly REM.

  • ≈1°C

    Fall in core body temperature around sleep onset. The wind-down is timed to help it along.

Published work behind the design

  1. Ngo, Martinetz, Born & Mölle (2013). Auditory closed-loop stimulation of the sleep slow oscillation enhances memory. Neuron 78(3).
  2. Papalambros et al. (2017). Acoustic enhancement of sleep slow oscillations and concomitant memory improvement in older adults. Frontiers in Human Neuroscience 11.
  3. Stanchina et al. (2005). The influence of white noise on sleep in subjects exposed to ICU noise. Sleep Medicine 6(5).

Summaries of lab studies, not a claim about Drift's own results. Drift is a wellness product, not a medical device.
