02The objectHalo H1 · Sheet 2 of 7

Take a Halo apart and find five pieces.

  1. 01Ring

    Turned from a single bar of aluminium, anodised silver or graphite. Turn it for volume, press it to play or pause. It is the only part of Halo that moves. Ø 232 mm, 64 g

  2. 02Lens

    A 133 mm woofer fires upward into a machined aluminium cone, which bends its output through 90° and spreads it evenly in every direction. Four microphones sit in its rim. 133 mm, 360°

  3. 03Driver core

    Six 28 mm silk-dome tweeters sit 60° apart, each on its own amplifier channel. The processor times them as one ring rather than six separate speakers. 6 × 28 mm

  4. 04Wool sleeve

    A seamless wool-blend sleeve, woven in Jutland and open to sound. It lifts off for cleaning, replacement or a change of colour. Wool 80 %, polyamide 20 %

  5. 05Oak foot

    Soap-finished oak, turned on a lathe, floating on a 6 mm shadow gap that keeps vibration out of the shelf. The serial number is burned into the underside. FSC oak, 640 g

Fig. 2 — Exploded view, model H1. The sleeve is drawn translucent so the driver core shows through.

03SoundHalo H1 · Sheet 3 of 7

Six tweeters face out. One woofer fires up into a lens.

A speaker that points at you sounds right in one chair. Halo is built so that the direct sound is the same in every direction, and the room does the rest.

3.1

Lens

The woofer leaves through a conical aluminium lens, so bass and lower midrange spread the same way in every direction instead of beaming at the front.

3.2

Ring of six

With six tweeters at fixed 60° intervals, the processor steers their phase to keep the pattern even above 2 kHz, where most round speakers begin to lobe.

3.3

Measured, then corrected

Four microphones in the lens rim measure the room. Modes between 38 and 400 Hz are cut, and the correction is stored for each position.

05RoomHalo H1 · Sheet 5 of 7

In a 5.2 by 4.1 metre living room, the level varies by 1.7 dB from dining table to doorway.

  1. R1

    Room-sense

    A sweep, four microphones, eleven seconds. Halo measures 38 to 400 Hz, cuts the modes that boom and stores the result for that spot.

  2. R2

    Placement memory

    On a shelf, in a corner or free on the floor, it recognises the boundary and adjusts bass gain by up to 6 dB.

  3. R3

    Stereo pair

    Two Halos one to four metres apart lock wirelessly to within 20 microseconds and play as left and right.

  4. R4

    Multi-room

    Group up to twelve over Wi-Fi 6, each with its own correction, playing in sample-accurate sync.

  5. R5

    Microphone switch

    A hardware slider breaks the microphone circuit. The microphones are on during calibration and at no other time.
