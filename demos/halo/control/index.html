(01) The idea

Most speakers point. They aim their sound at one spot on the sofa and leave the rest of the room to chance. Halo does the opposite. A ring of seven drivers releases sound in every direction at once, and the room does the rest — so the best seat in the house is every seat in the house.

  1. 01 — Form

    A single turned form.

    Ø218 by 340 millimetres. One aluminium crown, one seamless knitted sleeve and nothing between them that you would call a joint.

  2. 02 — Light

    A ring that breathes.

    Ninety-six warm LEDs sit behind an opal diffuser. The ring swells with the music, fades with the evening and never blinks for a notification.

  3. 03 — Touch

    Rest a hand on the crown.

    No buttons. A capacitive dial under the aluminium: turn for volume, tap to pause, hold to pass the music to the next room. Six microphones sit flush beside it.

  4. 04 — Ground

    Sound into the floor.

    A downward-firing woofer couples to the surface it stands on, while two opposed radiators cancel the vibration. The shelf stays quiet. The room does not.

(03) The sound technology

Seven voices, one circle.

Six full-range drivers form the ring. A long-throw woofer fires down into the floor. Seven discrete amplifiers keep every voice independent, so the speaker can shape its own dispersion instead of hoping for the best.

  1. 01

    The crown

    Turned from one aluminium billet. It holds the touch dial and six beam-forming microphones.

  2. 02

    The halo

    Ninety-six LEDs behind an opal light guide. Warm only: 2700 K, nothing else.

  3. 03

    The ring

    Six 50 mm full-range drivers, sixty degrees apart, each with its own 40 W amplifier.

  4. 04

    The chamber

    A sealed 3.1-litre cavity with two opposed 130 mm radiators. Their motion cancels, so the cabinet stays still.

  5. 05

    The woofer

    A 165 mm long-throw driver fires downward through a conical diffuser. 180 W, down to 32 Hz.

  6. 06

    The base

    A decoupled plinth with a rubberised ring keeps the speaker from talking to the furniture.

(04) Materials

Made to be touched.

Three materials and nothing else, each chosen for how it sounds and how it ages. If a part does not earn its place, it is not on the speaker.

A

Knitted in Herning

One seamless tube of wool and recycled polyester, knitted to shape on Danish looms. About 26,000 stitches and acoustically transparent, so the drivers behind it never feel veiled.

B

Turned, not stamped

Crown and base are turned from 100% recycled aluminium and anodised in-house. Each is finished by hand and laser-etched with the serial number of its speaker.

C

2700 kelvin, no more

The ring only ever shows warm light. No colour cycling, no animation for its own sake: it breathes with the music and sleeps with the room.

(05) Room-filling

Fills the room. Not the shelf.

Every room has its own acoustics: standing waves, dead corners, a wall that rings. On first power-up Halo plays a six-second sweep, listens with its six microphones and rewrites its own tuning for where you put it down.

Five things it does so you don’t have to

01 / 05

01

Listens before it plays

A six-second sweep and six microphones map your room. Adaptive Room Tuning rebuilds the filter set around the walls, the rug and the shelf behind it. Repeat it whenever you move the furniture.

02

Pairs in a breath

Place two Halos in a room, hold both crowns for a second and they become a true stereo pair, with channels assigned by the microphones. No cables, no app required.

03

Follows you from room to room

Hold the crown and the music moves to the nearest Halo, in sync to within a tenth of a millisecond. Link up to twelve speakers across the house.

04

Knows when to whisper

Night mode narrows dynamic range and rolls off the low end below what the neighbours can feel, while the ring dims to a single warm thread of light.

05

Plays everything, losslessly

AirPlay 2, Spotify Connect, Tidal Connect, Roon and Bluetooth LE Audio, up to 24-bit / 192 kHz over Wi-Fi 6 or Ethernet. Or plug in with USB-C.
