01Studio philosophy

We believe a public building should feel like a shared hearth. So we raise it from what grows nearby — spruce, larch and pine, felled within two hundred kilometres — and let it silver honestly with age.

Founded in 2009 by Ingrid Solheim and Anders Vik, Atelier Norr is a studio of twenty-two architects, model-makers and carpenters working between Oslo and Tromsø. We draw in wood because it is the only structural material that is grown by the sun, and the only one the north has in abundance.

Ingrid Solheim & Anders Vik Founding partners

  1. 01

    Timber first

    Every structure begins as a tree. We design for glulam, cross-laminated panels and hand-cut joinery before we consider anything else, and specify steel only where wood cannot reach.

  2. 02

    Public by design

    A civic building is a promise to a town. We start with the threshold: where people stop, meet, shelter from weather and linger together long after the programme is done.

  3. 03

    Long, slow time

    Details are drawn for a hundred years: replaceable cladding, honest weathering, repairable joints. The best carbon saving is a building nobody ever wants to demolish.

Embodied carbon

Lighter on the land.

Wood stores carbon the forest drew from the air. Compared with a conventional concrete-and-steel civic building of the same size, our buildings start life with roughly two-thirds less embodied carbon.

02Selected work

Five buildings, one grain.

Civic halls, a chapel, a school and a mountain home — each drawn for its place, built from trees grown within a day’s drive of the site. Open any project for the drawing and the numbers.

01

Bodø2023Civic

A reading room beneath five glulam arches, set where the old fish quay once stood.

Drawing & numbers
Client
Bodø Municipality
Area
4,200 m²
Timber
1,860 m³ spruce glulam & CLT
Embodied carbon
188 kg CO₂e/m²

The arches repeat at five scales, from a low entrance hall to a twenty-four metre reading nave. Each is a single glulam section, laminated in a sawmill ninety minutes down the coast and floated in on a barge. Inside, the arches filter the low arctic sun into a long, quiet gradient across the shelves.

The ground floor is a covered public street — open to the harbour and used for markets in winter. Cladding is untreated pine, designed to silver within six years.

02

Røros2021Chapel

A single slit of light in a larch A-frame, raised by twelve carpenters in eleven weeks.

Drawing & numbers
Client
Kvarv Parish Council
Area
210 m²
Timber
96 m³ local larch
Structure
Pegged A-frame, no steel

The chapel has no windows in the usual sense. One vertical opening, sixteen metres tall, follows the line of the frame and lets a blade of daylight travel across the floor between dawn and dusk. In the dark months the same slit becomes a lantern for the valley.

Every joint is hand-cut and pegged in larch. The building was designed to be taken apart, board by board, if the parish ever needs to move it.

03

Tromsø2024Civic

A sawtooth roof that opens to the north light — and to the aurora from a rooftop foyer.

Drawing & numbers
Client
City of Tromsø
Area
7,800 m²
Timber
3,950 m³ CLT & glulam
Embodied carbon
201 kg CO₂e/m²

Three timber saw-teeth cover a concert room, a gallery and a workshop hall. Each tooth is a long CLT slope facing the sun, with a vertical north-facing clerestory that floods the room with cool, even daylight and none of the glare.

Between November and January the clerestories glow from inside like a row of lanterns. The building was finished in 22 months, with the entire roof assembled indoors in a prefabrication hall and craned into place in nine days.

04

Hemsedal2022Home

A family house that leans into the slope, with a roof that gathers snow for insulation and shelters a south-facing deck.

Drawing & numbers
Client
Private family
Area
285 m²
Timber
140 m³ pine & spruce
Energy
Passive-house certified

A single mono-pitch roof runs from a low north edge to a high glazed south corner, so winter sun reaches the kitchen table at noon while the long north side stays tight against the wind.

The cladding is vertical pine boards, left unfinished. The family knows to expect a silver coat in about five winters — and has already chosen to keep it.

05

Lillehammer2020School

A school built like a forest: columns in rows, classrooms between, and a courtyard of birches.

Drawing & numbers
Client
Lillehammer Municipality
Area
5,600 m²
Timber
2,300 m³ spruce frame
Pupils
420, ages 6 to 15

Classrooms are open frames of glulam on a 4.5 metre grid. Walls can be moved, glazed or solid, and the school has already been reorganised twice by its teachers without an architect in the room.

The Falu-red cladding is traditional linseed paint made from the same mineral pigment as the farm buildings nearby. Repainting every fourteen years is part of the caretakers’ handbook.

03Process

From forest to threshold, in five movements.

  1. Movement 01 / 05

    Listen

    We walk the site with the people who will use it — in winter, if we can. Local carpenters, foresters and neighbours join the first workshops, because they know where the wind turns and where the snow stays.

    Weeks 1–6Site walksCommunity workshops

  2. Movement 02 / 05

    Read the site

    Sun paths, snow load, soil, sight lines. We draw the land before we draw the building, then place the footprint where it will earn its warmth — and keep every tree we can.

    Weeks 6–12Solar & wind studyFootprint

  3. Movement 03 / 05

    Draft in timber

    Structure and architecture are drawn as one. Glulam frames, cross-laminated panels and joinery are modelled to the millimetre with our engineers and the sawmill that will cut them.

    Weeks 12–30Frame & joineryMock-ups

  4. Movement 04 / 05

    Prefabricate

    Panels are machined and assembled in a covered hall, numbered, wrapped and trucked north. Weather delays shrink; accuracy and craft rise.

    Months 8–14Off-site buildQuality walk-throughs

  5. Movement 05 / 05

    Raise & tend

    A frame goes up in days, not months. We stay after handover: oiling, mending and learning how the building ages — then we write that back into our details.

    Months 14–24Annual care visitPost-occupancy study
