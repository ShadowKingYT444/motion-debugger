01 — Studio philosophy

We believe a building should be the slowest thing in its landscape. We draw in timber because it remembers: every ring a winter survived, every joint a conversation between carpenter and architect. We design for the hundred years after the ribbon is cut.

i.

Grown, not extracted

Our frames are glulam from managed Nordic forest, felled within 200 km of the site. Every building stores more carbon than it took to make.

ii.

Honest joints

Structure is the architecture. We leave the frame visible, cut the connections by hand-checked CNC, and never hide a bolt behind plasterboard.

iii.

Built for the long dark

Low winter sun, deep snow, short days. We shape every room around the light that is there, not the light we wish we had.

02 — Anatomy of a hall

The frame is the building.

01 / 04

Plinth

Granite footings lift the timber 300 mm off the ground. Dry feet are the whole secret to a five-hundred-year frame.

02 / 04

Frame

Glulam columns, a tie beam and paired rafters, raised in four days. Each joint is cut to a tolerance of a millimetre in our partner mill.

03 / 04

Skin

Open-spaced larch boards left to silver. The frame still reads through the rainscreen; the weather does the finishing.

04 / 04

Light

One wall of glass turns to the low southern sun. The hall holds its heat, and the timber glows from the inside.

04 — Process

From the first walk to the tenth winter.

01

Listen

We spend a week with the people who will use the building, and a day in the weather that will use it harder. Briefs are written together, in pencil.

Weeks 1–3
02

Read the site

Sun paths, snow load, wind, soil and the line of the old fence. We survey in winter, when the ground is hardest to flatter.

Weeks 3–8
03

Draw & model

Every scheme is built at 1:50 in birch ply before it is drawn on a screen. Light studies, load paths and joinery are tested by hand and by software.

Months 2–6
04

Mill

Glulam beams are cut to the millimetre in a mill an hour from the site, then dry-fitted in the yard. Nothing arrives that has not already been assembled once.

Months 6–12
05

Raise & return

The frame goes up in days, not months. We come back at one, five and ten years to listen again, oil the timber and learn what to draw differently.
