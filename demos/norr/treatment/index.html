I Studio philosophy

A town hall should outlast the people who first voted in it. So we build with the one structural material that grows back, and we draw every joint so that a carpenter two hundred years from now can read it, repair it and trust it.

I Wood from within 200 km

Spruce and pine felled in the valleys around the site, so every beam can be traced to a forest and a stamp.

Median haul 118 km

II Joints before fixings

Wood meets wood in scarfs, tenons and dovetails. Steel appears only where the structural calculation asks for it.

Steel in 4% of joints

III One room for the whole town

Every civic building we draw keeps one warm, lit room open to everyone, without a ticket or a reason to be there.

III Process

From a plot to a roof in four moves.

  1. I

    Read the site

    We walk the plot in winter and in summer, log the sun, wind and snow, and mark the trees we keep. Sills sit on stone pads, never in a slab.

  2. II

    Raise the frame

    Posts and tie beams are milled from dried spruce, cut to joinery by machine and checked by hand, then stood in a day.

  3. III

    Close the roof

    Glulam rafters carry a 16 m clear span to a ridge beam. No steel tie crosses the room.

  4. IIII

    Clad and keep

    Pine-tar and falu-red boards go on last. A maintenance plan is handed over with the keys.
