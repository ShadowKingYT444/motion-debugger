Why Halo

Most speakers point at you. We thought music should arrive the way light does in a good room: from everywhere, without a direction. So we built a ring, tuned a room, and left out everything that shouts.

Lydværk, Nordhavn, Copenhagen
The object

One piece. No front.

A 214 mm cylinder with nothing to aim. Place it on a shelf, a sideboard or the floor and the sound simply fills the space around it.

A ring of light.

The halo is the interface. Trace it with a fingertip to change volume, tap to play and pause. It glows with the level and fades when you leave the room.

Knitted to shape.

The sleeve is a single seamless wool knit, made on a flat-bed machine in Jutland and drawn over the acoustic frame. It breathes, so the sound does too.

Grounded in oak.

A turned oak foot carries the downward-firing woofer and decouples it from your furniture. Nothing buzzes. The shelf stays still.

Sound technology

A speaker that listens first.

Six micro-drivers sit in a ring, 60 degrees apart, and a long-throw woofer fires down into the foot. Four microphones then map your walls and furniture and bend the output to suit. Move the speaker below and watch the room answer.
