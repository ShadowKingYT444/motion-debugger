The idea

Most speakers point at you. Halo surrounds you. Seven drivers, one acoustic lens and a ring of light that tells you the room is listening.

01 — The object

Four parts.
Nothing wasted.

Halo is built the way a Danish chair is: a small number of honest parts, each doing exactly one job, assembled by hand in a single studio on Nørrebro. Keep scrolling and we will take it apart.

Part one — The ring

A ring of light that listens.

A single ring of micro-LEDs behind a frosted aluminium lip. It brightens with volume, breathes when idle and answers touch: slide a finger around it to change the level.

Elements
128 LEDs
Control
Capacitive
Part two — The sleeve

Knitted, not stretched.

A seamless acoustic knit made from recycled wool-blend yarn, three-dimensionally knitted in one piece. Open enough to be invisible to the sound, dense enough to hold its shape for a decade.

Part three — The array

Seven drivers, one lens.

Six 1.2-inch full-range drivers ring the core, firing into a conical acoustic lens that turns direct sound into one even 360° wavefront. A 5-inch long-throw woofer fires down into the base.

A solid aluminium disc, spun and anodised, anchors everything. It lowers the centre of gravity, soaks up resonance and carries heat away from the amplifiers.

02 — Sound technology

One lens. Three hundred and sixty degrees.

Top-down view
Illustrative model
360°Even coverage
±1.4 dBVariation between seats

A conventional speaker beams its sound forward. Everything off axis loses treble and presence, so the best seat in the room is the one right in front of it. Halo fires sound upward and lets a lens place it evenly in every direction.

A

Acoustic lens

A machined cone spreads the six drivers into a single wavefront with under 1.5 dB of variation around the full circle.

B

Room-aware DSP

Four microphones listen to the room, find the reflections that hurt the sound and cancel them before they reach you.

C

Phase-coherent array

Every driver is timed to the microsecond, so a voice stays a point in space rather than smearing across the room.

Flat where it matters.

04 — Rooms

It fills the room. Then it disappears.

01 / 05
01Calibration

It listens first.

Four microphones in the base map your room in nine seconds: the walls, the shelves, the sofa you forgot to mention. Then it tunes the array to fit.

02Multi-room

Stereo, without the wires.

Place two Halos anywhere within fifteen metres. They find each other, lock to within 20 microseconds and become a true pair. Or join up to eight rooms.

03Touch

Touch the halo.

Slide a finger around the ring to set the volume. Tap to pause, double-tap to skip. No app needed for any of it.

04Battery

A day and a half of music.

Twenty hours on a charge, and 0 to 80% in 70 minutes over USB-C. Plug it into the wall and it never has to decide.

05Whisper mode

Full sound, small volume.

After 22:00 Halo narrows its dynamic range and softens its low end, so it stays full at a level the neighbours won't hear. In Copenhagen, they do hear.
