A short manifesto

Most speakers point at you. Halo surrounds you. We gave six drivers a single centre, and let the room do the rest. No hub, no app to open, no cables to hide. Just one object that disappears into the evening and fills it.

The object

01

One seam of light.

A 6 mm aperture, cut from a single turned block of aluminium. It breathes with the music, dims with the evening, and tells you what the room is doing. Nothing more.

02

Wool that lets sound through.

Knitted in Jutland on a loom older than the company. Open enough to be acoustically invisible, dense enough to hide everything behind it. Lift it off, wash it, put it back.

03

Six drivers, one centre.

Four silk-dome mid-tweeters at 90 degrees, an upward-firing waveguide and a 5.25 inch down-firing woofer, all sharing one axis. The sound has nowhere to be but around you.

04

Three points of oak.

An oiled solid-oak foot decouples bass from your shelf. Move Halo from a table to a windowsill and it re-tunes itself in ten seconds.

Materials

Three materials. Nothing to hide.

A

Knitted wool

A seamless tube, knitted in one piece from undyed Danish wool and natural pigments. Acoustically transparent, naturally flame-retardant, and it ages like a good jumper.

B

Turned aluminium

Machined from solid billet, bead-blasted, then anodised by hand in small batches. The cap is the only part of Halo you touch, so it is the part we got most wrong first.

C

Oiled oak

FSC-certified oak from a sawmill outside Roskilde, finished with plant oil and wax. Every foot is cut from a different board, so no two grains match.

A ten-second sweep through its own microphone array maps the walls, the shelf, and the sofa you keep meaning to move. Halo tunes itself to match. Do it again whenever you rearrange.
