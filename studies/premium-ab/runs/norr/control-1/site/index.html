01 — Studio philosophy

A building should belong to its place the way a tree does. We draw with the grain of the land, the light of the north and the hands of the people who will keep it. Timber is not our material of choice. It is our way of thinking: renewable, warm, repairable, and honest about time.

i.

Grow, don’t extract

Every beam is sourced from managed forests within 200 kilometres. Each building stores more carbon than it emits to raise.

ii.

Draw with light

At 69° north the sun is a season, not an hour. We shape rooms around its low angle, its absence, and its return.

iii.

Design to be repaired

Joints you can see, boards you can replace. Our buildings are made to weather, then to be mended, not retired.

03 — Process

From forestto threshold.

Site & listening
  1. 01

    Listen to the site

    We walk the land across a full season before drawing a line: wind, snowmelt, sightlines, the path people already take. The first sketch is always a plan of what we leave alone.

    6–12 weeks
  2. 02

    Draw & source

    Models, full-scale joint mock-ups and a timber schedule written alongside the plans. We name the forest, the sawmill and the trees before we name the rooms.

    4–9 months
  3. 03

    Raise the frame

    Prefabricated in a covered workshop, delivered numbered, erected in days. Quiet sites, short cranes, no concrete beyond the footings that need it.

    3–10 weeks
  4. 04

    Clad & finish

    Larch, pine and spruce boards, oiled by hand. Each facade is detailed to silver gracefully, and every fixing is reachable for the day it needs replacing.

    2–5 months
  5. 05

    Inhabit & tend

    We return after the first winter and every fifth year, with oil, a ladder and a notebook. A building is only finished when its keepers say it is.

    Ongoing
