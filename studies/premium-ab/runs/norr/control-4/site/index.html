01 Studio philosophy

We believe a building should grow old the way a forest does — slowly, gracefully, and in service of everyone who gathers beneath it. Timber is not a style for us. It is a promise about time, carbon and care.

i.

Site before form

We spend a winter on every plot before drawing. Where the snow settles, where the light lands at 3 pm in December — the building begins there.

ii.

Honest structure

Columns, beams and joints are the architecture. Nothing is hidden behind plasterboard that cannot be admired in the open.

iii.

Civic generosity

Every library, school and hall we make gives more to the street than it takes: shelter, warmth, and a reason to linger.

03 Our process

From first snow
to last nail.

Listen

  1. 01

    Listen

    Winter site studies, daylight diaries, conversations with the people who will use the building. We measure snow load, wind and shadow before we draw a single line.

    4 – 8 weeks
  2. 02

    Draw

    Hand sketches become card models, then digital twins. Every iteration is judged by how it feels at human height, in low sun, in snow.

    8 – 14 weeks
  3. 03

    Frame

    Our in-house engineers detail each joint for CNC fabrication in partner workshops. Timber is cut to a tolerance of one millimetre, numbered and flat-packed for the site.

    12 – 20 weeks
  4. 04

    Raise

    The frame of a typical building rises in days, not months — quiet, dry, and with almost no waste. We are on site with the builders every week.

    2 – 5 weeks frame
  5. 05

    Keep

    We stay. Moisture sensors, annual inspections and a care manual written for the next generation of caretakers — so the building ages well.

    Lifetime of the building
