01Studio philosophy

We believe a building is a slow conversation between a forest, a climate and a community. We choose timber because it remembers: every season written into the grain, every hand into the joint. Our work is quiet by intent — calm light, honest structure, and rooms that make people linger.

Material honesty

Structure is the finish. Glulam ribs, CLT walls and pegged joints are left visible, so a building explains how it stands.

Light as a material

At 69°N the sun is a guest. We shape sections and apertures around low angles, long dusks and the blue hour.

Built to be repaired

We design for a hundred and fifty years: demountable details, replaceable cladding, and carbon stored, not spent.

—Material study

A spruce takes a hundred years to become a beam.

We are patient with that. Each ring is a year of weather; we design the building to honour it, and plant three for every one we use.

03Process
Stage 01 / 05
  1. 01

    Listen to the site

    We walk the land through a full season where we can: tracing wind, snow load, sun paths and the routes people already take. The brief is written by the place as much as the client.

    4–8 weeks
  2. 02

    Sketch & model

    Hand drawings and physical models in spruce and birch ply — we test volume, shadow and approach before anything is digitised.

    6–12 weeks
  3. 03

    Engineer the frame

    With timber engineers we resolve spans, connections and fire strategy so structure and architecture are one decision, not two.

    3–6 months
  4. 04

    Prefabricate

    Members are cut to the millimetre in dry workshops, numbered and shipped flat. Less waste, less noise on site, a tighter envelope.

    4–9 months
  5. 05

    Raise & return

    A frame can rise in days. We stay for the first winter — and return each year to oil, mend and listen to how it ages.

    Ongoing
