Studio philosophy

We believe a building should be the slowest thing in a city. So we build it from the slowest material we know — spruce that took a century to grow, joined by hand, and meant to outlast the people who ordered it.

Three habits of the studio

  1. 01

    Slow material

    Timber stores carbon while it stands. We specify wood from forests within 200 km, milled to the dimension of the building, not the catalogue.

  2. 02

    Honest joints

    Every connection is drawn to be seen: dovetails, steel-free halvings, pegs. A visitor should be able to read how the roof is carried.

  3. 03

    Long light

    North of 60° the sun is a rare guest. We shape sections around its low arc, so a winter noon fills a hall rather than grazing it.

Process

From a winter of looking to a frame in days

01

Read the site

We spend a winter before we draw: sun angles, snow load, wind, the old paths people already walk.

02

Draw with the grain

Each member is drawn from a real log — glulam posts, cross-laminated panels, joints you can read.

03

Raise the frame

Prefabricated in our Steinkjer workshop, the frame goes up in days. Dry, quiet, mostly without cranes.

04

Live in it

Light changes a building every hour. We return after a year with a tape measure and a thermal camera.
