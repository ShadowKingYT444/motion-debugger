01Studio philosophy

We believe a building should be a slow, good conversation between a forest, a place and the people who will live in it for a hundred years. So we draw in section, we choose timber that was grown within five hundred kilometres, and we design every joint so that it can be opened, repaired and understood.

Material honesty

Structure is the finish. Glulam columns, CLT walls and larch cladding are left to show how the building stands, and how it will weather.

Civic generosity

Libraries, schools and halls should give more room than the brief asks for: a deep porch, a warm corner, a stair worth lingering on.

Slow growth

We take on eight projects a year. Each one is walked in every season and drawn at 1:5 before it is milled, then followed for a decade.

03How a building is made

From a walked site
to a raised frame.

  1. 01

    Listen

    We walk the site through a season: sun, wind, snowline, the paths people already cut across it.

  2. 02

    Draw

    Section before plan. Every joint is drawn at 1:5 before a single beam is ordered.

  3. 03

    Mill

    Spruce and larch from Nordic forests are glued, CNC-milled and numbered at our partner mill.

  4. 04

    Raise

    A frame goes up in days, not months. The house is weathertight before the first coffee is made.
